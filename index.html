Con Safe Sip

Cover your drink. Watch your friends.

A community safety initiative making drink protection easier to access at conventions, parties, and meetups — so more people know how to look out for drink spiking and stay safer together.

How to join How it works

What is Con Safe Sip?

Looking out for each other, one cover at a time

After a frightening situation at a convention involving someone close to us, we started Con Safe Sip — a small community safety initiative focused on helping congoers better protect their drinks.

Members carry individually packaged drink covers and offer them to people who need one at conventions, parties, meetups, and other social events — including anime cons, comic cons, and beyond. The goal is simple: make drink protection easier to access, share practical tips for staying safer at a con (including awareness of roofies and other drink-spiking drugs), encourage people to look out for each other without judgment, and hopefully prevent someone else from experiencing the same thing.

Con drink safety

Look out for each other — never blame the victim

Getting drugged is never your fault. Not for what you drank, what you wore, who you talked to, or whether you used a cover. The only person responsible for drink spiking is the person who did it.

Con Safe Sip will never blame, shame, or second-guess someone who was spiked or who thinks they might have been. Asking for a cover, accepting help, or reporting what happened is brave — not something to apologize for.

A few practical habits that can make conventions, parties, and other events a little safer for everyone:

  • Cover your drink when you can — and feel free to ask a Con Safe Sip member for a free cover
  • Don’t leave drinks unattended, and don’t accept open drinks from strangers
  • Stick with friends you trust, and check in on each other through the night
  • Watch for sudden, unusual impairment — in yourself or someone else — that doesn’t match how much they drank
  • If something feels wrong, trust that feeling. Get to a safe place with people you trust and get help
  • Never pressure anyone to drink, leave their friends, or “just finish” a drink they don’t feel okay about

Drink covers and testing tools are one extra layer — not a test of whether someone “did safety right.” Community care means believing people, helping without judgment, and making protection easy to ask for.

Know the risks

Common substances used to spike drinks

“Roofies” is a catch-all people use for drugs slipped into drinks without consent. You usually can’t taste, smell, or see them. Knowing what they can do helps you spot when something is wrong — in yourself or a friend.

  • GHB (and related chemicals like GBL) Can cause sudden drowsiness, dizziness, confusion, nausea, memory gaps, loss of muscle control, or passing out. Effects can hit fast and get much worse mixed with alcohol. Breathing can slow dangerously.
  • Rohypnol (flunitrazepam) and other benzos The original “roofie,” plus drugs like Xanax or Valium crushed into a drink. They sedate, relax muscles, cloud thinking, and often cause blackouts — someone may look “just really drunk” when they barely drank.
  • Ketamine Can cause dissociation (feeling detached from your body), numbness, confusion, trouble moving or speaking, and memory loss. People may stare blankly, stumble, or seem suddenly far more impaired than their drinks explain.
  • Other meds & mixes Prescription sleep aids, muscle relaxants, and other pills get used the same way. Alcohol itself is also used to incapacitate people. Drinks and pills are sometimes laced with opioids (including fentanyl) — you often won’t know what’s in them.

Red flags: sudden, unusual impairment that doesn’t match how much someone drank; confusion; vomiting; can’t stay awake; can’t walk steadily; or they seem “gone” while everyone else is fine. Trust that feeling and get help — you don’t need to know which drug it was.

Important safety note

Drink covers help. They are not a guarantee.

Drink covers can reduce opportunities for tampering, but no product can completely prevent drink spiking. Covers and drink-testing products should never be treated as a guarantee that a drink is safe — and not using one is never a reason to blame someone.

If you believe you or someone else may have been drugged:

  • Stop drinking immediately
  • Stay with trusted friends
  • Contact convention security or venue staff
  • Seek medical help as soon as possible
  • Do not leave the person alone
  • Call emergency services if they are unconscious, struggling to breathe, having a seizure, or cannot be awakened

Narcan (naloxone)

Narcan is a medicine that can reverse an opioid overdose (including fentanyl, heroin, and many pain pills). It will not reverse GHB, Rohypnol, ketamine, benzos, or alcohol on their own — but that does not mean you should wait to use it when someone is in trouble.

If you’re unsure what they took, still give Narcan. People lace drinks and other drugs with opioids. Narcan won’t harm someone who didn’t take opioids, and it can save a life if they did. Call 911, stay with them, and give another dose if they don’t wake up or their breathing stays slow — follow the instructions on the package.

Signs that may mean an opioid overdose: slow or stopped breathing, blue or gray lips/fingernails, gurgling or snoring sounds, limp body, or they won’t wake up. Get emergency help either way — Narcan buys time; it is not a substitute for medical care.

Con Safe Sip does not replace security, medical care, or watching out for your friends. It is just one more layer of protection, and one more way we can take care of each other — without judgment.

Help & resources

Where to get support

You don’t have to figure this out alone. These are places to start — for emergencies, aftercare, and carrying Narcan.

  • Emergencies: Call 911 (or your local emergency number) if someone is unconscious, can’t breathe normally, is seizing, or you can’t wake them.
  • RAINN (sexual assault support): rainn.org · hotline 800-656-HOPE (4673) · 24/7 chat available on their site
  • Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222 · poison.org
  • SAMHSA National Helpline (substance use & mental health): 1-800-662-HELP (4357) · samhsa.gov
  • Find Narcan / naloxone near you: CDC naloxone info · many pharmacies and health departments offer it over the counter or free — ask locally or check your state’s naloxone finder
  • Stamp Out Spiking (drink spiking awareness & victim support): stampoutspiking.org · prevention info, training, and resources if you or someone else has been spiked
  • Convention / venue: Tell security or medical staff if you think someone was spiked. Ask a friend to stay with you.

Con Safe Sip shares this for community awareness. We are not medical professionals — when in doubt, call emergency services.
